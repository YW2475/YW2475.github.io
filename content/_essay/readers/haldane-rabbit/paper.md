**Original:** THE STORY OF A SKIRMISH IN THE WAR ON DISEASE

**中文:** 抗击疾病战争中的小冲突的故事

<a id="S002"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** MOST educated people have a rough but fairly accurate idea of the methods employed by the bacteriologist in fighting disease. But in many cases we cannot deal directly with the invading organisms, or they have already done irreparable damage by the time the first symptoms appear. Often, too, the cause of sickness is an unusual demand on the body’s resources, such as pregnancy or the very rapid growth of babies; and under such conditions constitutional weakness or unsatisfactory diet may lead to serious results.

**中文:** 大多数受过教育的人，对细菌学家如何同疾病作战，都有一个粗略而大体准确的认识。但在许多情况下，我们无法直接对付入侵的生物；或者，等到最初的症状出现时，它们已经造成无法挽回的损害。此外，疾病往往源于身体的机能受到超乎寻常的要求，例如怀孕或婴儿的迅速生长；在这些情况下，体质虚弱或饮食不良，都可能导致严重后果。

<a id="S003"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** In all these cases we need a knowledge of how the body works, and how to supplement its resources. If the kidney has been damaged we can often put the patient on a diet which gives it so little work that it can still carry out its functions. If the part of the pancreas which makes insulin has been destroyed, we give the patient daily injections of insulin from the pancreas of pigs. And very often, if we can only relieve the symptoms and keep the patient healthy, the body exercises its marvellous capacity for recovery. The surgeon puts a broken leg in splints. The biochemist provides, so to say, chemical splints for damaged organs.

**中文:** 在所有这些情况下，我们需要了解身体如何运作以及如何补充其资源。如果肾脏受损，我们通常可以让患者节食，这样肾脏的工作量就很小，因此仍然可以发挥其功能。如果产生胰岛素的胰腺部分被破坏，我们就会每天给患者注射来自猪胰腺的胰岛素。很多时候，如果我们只能缓解症状并保持患者健康，身体就会发挥其惊人的恢复能力。外科医生将断腿装上夹板。可以说，生物化学家为受损器官提供了化学夹板。

<a id="S004"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** The story that I have to tell is of the discovery of an improved method of treatment for a rather unimportant disease, a discovery in which I happened to play a part. It began, like most scientific work, with the investigation of a very abstract problem, and the original workers had no idea whatever of how their results would be applied to practical medicine.

**中文:** 我要讲述的故事是关于一种治疗相当不重要的疾病的改进方法的发现，而我碰巧参与了这一发现。与大多数科学工作一样，它始于对一个非常抽象问题的调查，最初的工作人员根本不知道他们的结果将如何应用于实际医学。

<a id="S005"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** I came into the story with no humanitarian motives. I wanted to find out what happened to a man when one made him more acid or more alkaline. The chemists told me that my body was a system of negatively charged colloids. They also told me that when one makes such a system more alkaline the electrical charge on the colloids increases, and that when one makes it more acid it diminishes. But they had apparently never wondered what a colloidal system felt like when one diminished its charge.

**中文:** 我进入这个故事并没有任何人道主义动机。我想知道当一个人变得更酸或更碱性时会发生什么。化学家告诉我，我的身体是一个带负电的胶体系统。他们还告诉我，当使这样的系统变得更加碱性时，胶体上的电荷会增加，而当使系统变得更加酸性时，胶体上的电荷就会减少。但他们显然从未想过当胶体系统的电荷减少时会是什么样的感觉。

<a id="S006"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** One might, of course, have tried experiments on a rabbit first, and some work had been done along those lines; but it is difficult to be sure how a rabbit feels at any time. Indeed, many rabbits make no serious attempt to co-operate with one. I except always a large buck called Boanerges (which is, being interpreted, the Son of Thunder). Boanerges had to breathe carbon monoxide every day. He sat on the table with his nose in a well-greased funnel. When he got bored he stamped. That was before the war, so no doubt the noise impressed me more than it would now, but I seem to remember that any glass one left on the table collapsed into rather fine dust. If one took no notice of his first stamp he proceeded to walk off. However, he was always willing to co-operate for such a period as he thought reasonable; but most rabbits get frightened, and to do the sort of things to a dog that one does to the average medical student requires a licence signed in triplicate by two archbishops, as far as I can remember.

**中文:** 当然，人们可能首先在兔子身上尝试过实验，并且已经按照这些思路做了一些工作。但很难随时确定兔子的感受。事实上，许多兔子并没有认真尝试与一只兔子合作。我总是除了一只名叫Boanerges的大雄鹿（被解释为雷霆之子）。博内吉斯每天都必须呼吸一氧化碳。他坐在桌子上，鼻子伸进抹了油的漏斗里。当他感到无聊时，他就会跺脚。那是在战争之前，所以毫无疑问，噪音给我留下的印象比现在更深刻，但我似乎记得留在桌子上的任何玻璃都碎成相当细的灰尘。如果有人没有注意到他的第一枚印记，他就会走开。不过，他总是愿意在他认为合理的期限内合作；但大多数兔子都会受到惊吓，据我所知，要对一只狗做像对普通医科学生那样的事情，需要有两位大主教签署的一式三份的许可证。

<a id="S007"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** A human colleague and I therefore began experiments on one another. Before relating what happened in these tests, it may be as well to discuss briefly the chemical facts with which we had to deal. Acid substances are acid because when dissolved in water they break up so as to yield hydrogen ions, that is to say, atoms of hydrogen which have lost their one electron, and thus acquired a positive charge. Pure water contains a few hydrogen ions—to be accurate, one part in ten thousand million by weight—and the concentration of hydrogen ions is greater than this in acid solutions, less in alkaline. These small concentrations cannot be measured directly, but are estimated by conductivity measurements, by the electro-motive force developed by hydrogen going into solution from a platinum electrode, or by means of colour changes in certain organic substances.

**中文:** 因此，我和一位人类同事开始互相进行实验。在讲述这些测试中发生的情况之前，最好先简要讨论一下我们必须处理的化学事实。酸性物质之所以是酸性，是因为当溶解在水中时，它们会分解产生氢离子，也就是说，氢原子失去了一个电子，从而获得了正电荷。纯水含有少量的氢离子——准确地说，是重量的万分之一——并且氢离子的浓度在酸性溶液中高于此浓度，在碱性溶液中低于此浓度。这些小浓度不能直接测量，而是通过电导率测量、氢气从铂电极进入溶液产生的电动势或通过某些有机物质的颜色变化来估计。

<a id="S008"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** The importance of hydrogen ion concentrations near neutrality was first realized by biochemists. It was found that the hydrogen ion concentration in the human blood was extraordinarily steady, being just on the alkaline side of neutrality. In fact, except for occasional abnormal people, it is doubtful if any variations at all from the usual value have been observed in healthy human beings at rest. The most alkaline healthy blood on record belonged to a conscientious objector! Each tissue seems to have its own normal hydrogen ion concentration. As soon as the constancy of these concentrations was discovered it became of interest to see, firstly how they were kept steady, and secondly what happened if they went wrong. The two are really bound up together, because among the most striking effects of an upset are the body’s efforts to remedy it.

**中文:** 生物化学家首先认识到氢离子浓度接近中性的重要性。结果发现，人体血液中的氢离子浓度非常稳定，正好处于中性的碱性一侧。事实上，除了偶尔出现异常的人之外，在健康人休息时是否观察到与正常值有任何差异是值得怀疑的。有记录以来碱性最强的健康血液属于依良心拒服兵役者！每个组织似乎都有自己的正常氢离子浓度。一旦发现这些浓度的恒定性，人们就开始感兴趣的是，首先它们是如何保持稳定的，其次是如果它们出错会发生什么。两者确实紧密相连，因为心烦意乱最显着的影响之一就是身体努力补救。

<a id="S009"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** It was learned that the most rapid means of regulating neutrality was through the breathing. The lungs supply the body with oxygen, and remove the carbon dioxide formed by the oxidation of food. The breathing is not regulated by need for oxygen, for a small decrease in the oxygen of the air breathed does not increase the ventilation of the lungs appreciably, nor does any increase slow it down. This is because the blood leaving the lungs is already almost saturated with oxygen, and an increase in the lung ventilation gets hardly any more in, nor does a small decrease appreciably lower the uptake. The chief effects of a changed rate of breathing are on the amount of carbon dioxide (or carbonic acid) lost per minute, and it is the amount of carbon dioxide in the blood which normally regulates the breathing.

**中文:** 据了解，调节中立性最快速的方法是通过呼吸。肺部为身体提供氧气，并去除食物氧化形成的二氧化碳。呼吸不受氧气需求的调节，因为呼吸空气中氧气的少量减少不会明显增加肺部通气量，任何增加也不会减慢肺部通气量。这是因为离开肺部的血液已经几乎饱和了氧气，肺部通气量的增加几乎不再进入，小幅减少也不会明显降低吸收量。呼吸速率变化的主要影响是每分钟损失的二氧化碳（或碳酸）量，而血液中的二氧化碳量通常调节呼吸。

<a id="S010"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** The kidneys also help to keep the tissues neutral by excreting excess of acid or alkali, but their action is far slower. It is their function to remove from the blood which passes through them substances which are foreign to it, or which are present in excess of certain standard amounts. Human blood is generally a little too acid as the sulphur and phosphorus of our foodstuffs are oxidized to sulphuric and phosphoric acids in the body. All the former and about half the latter are excreted in combination with ammonia, which is formed in the kidneys as required to neutralize the acids.

**中文:** 肾脏还通过排出过量的酸或碱来帮助保持组织中性，但其作用要慢得多。它们的功能是从流经它们的血液中去除外来物质或超过一定标准量的物质。人类的血液通常有点太酸，因为食物中的硫和磷在体内被氧化成硫酸和磷酸。所有前者和大约一半后者与氨结合排出体外，氨是根据中和酸的需要在肾脏中形成的。

<a id="S011"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** One of the experiments designed to show that carbon dioxide is the normal regulator of breathing was as follows. The subject breathed as fast and deep as he could for two or three minutes. After this he had no desire to breathe for some time, until in fact most of the carbon dioxide blown out had accumulated again owing to the constant steady oxidation in the tissues. By this time he was often blue in the face with oxygen want. These experiments were made to test the method by which breathing is regulated, and those who carried them out were more worried than interested by certain extra effects which they noticed. After about, half a minute they got violent ‘pins and needles’ in the hands, feet, and face, and after three or four their hands became curiously stiff, and sometimes their wrists bent involuntarily.

**中文:** 旨在证明二氧化碳是呼吸的正常调节剂的实验之一如下。受试者尽可能快而深地呼吸两三分钟。此后，他有一段时间没有呼吸的欲望，直到事实上，由于组织中不断稳定的氧化，大部分喷出的二氧化碳又重新积累起来。这时他经常脸色发青，缺氧。这些实验是为了测试调节呼吸的方法，进行这些实验的人对他们注意到的某些额外影响更多的是担心而不是感兴趣。大约半分钟后，他们的手、脚、脸就感到剧烈的“针刺感”，三四分钟后，他们的手变得奇怪地僵硬，有时手腕不由自主地弯曲。

<a id="S012"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** In 1920 Collip (who afterwards co-operated with Banting in the isolation of insulin) and Backus, in Alberta, Canada, noticed that the symptoms produced by forced breathing were largely those of slight tetany. Tetany, which must not be confused with tetanus, is a disease characterized by a cramp of the hands, feet, face, and sometimes the windpipe. It occurs in babies (generally in conjunction with rickets); in pregnant women; in adults whose parathyroid glands (four bodies in the neck, each about the size of a pea) have been injured, removed, or diseased; in diseases characterized by chronic vomiting, and sometimes for no obvious cause. It is much commoner in Germany and Austria than in this country. This was so even before the war, but since then it has greatly increased among children in Central Europe, owing to their unsatisfactory diet.

**中文:** 1920年，加拿大艾伯塔省的科利普（后来与班廷合作分离胰岛素）和巴克斯注意到，用力呼吸产生的症状主要是轻微的手足搐搦症。手足搐搦症切勿与破伤风混淆，是一种以手、脚、面部、有时甚至气管抽筋为特征的疾病。它发生在婴儿身上（通常与佝偻病同时发生）；孕妇；甲状旁腺（颈部有四个腺体，每个大约有豌豆大小）已受伤、被切除或患病的成年人；以慢性呕吐为特征的疾病，有时没有明显原因。它在德国和奥地利比在这个国家更常见。即使在战前也是如此，但自那以后，由于饮食不理想，中欧儿童的这种情况大大增加。

<a id="S013"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** At about the same time Grant and Goldman, of Washington University, breathed harder and longer than Collip, and obtained all the symptoms of tetany. Poor Goldman on one occasion, after about half an hour, uttered a shrill cry and went into a general convulsion. Every muscle in his body was contracted, his limbs stretched out stiff, and his back arched.

**中文:** 大约在同一时间，华盛顿大学的格兰特和戈德曼的呼吸比科利普更困难、更长，并出现了手足搐搦症的所有症状。可怜的戈德曼有一次，大约半小时后，发出一声尖叫，全身抽搐。他全身的每一块肌肉都在收缩，四肢僵硬地伸展着，背部拱起。

<a id="S014"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** I have never had a general convulsion as a result of self-experiments in over-breathing. My star turn is probably intense sweating, which breaks out after about twenty minutes. I also probably hold the endurance record of one and a half hours’ continuous spasm of the hands and face, though on that occasion I never breathed so hard as to cause the cramp to spread above the elbow, as it does in severe tetany.

**中文:** 我从来没有因为过度呼吸的自我实验而出现过全身抽搐。我的明星转变可能是大量出汗，大约二十分钟后就会出现。我也可能保持着手部和面部持续痉挛一个半小时的耐力记录，尽管那次我从未呼吸过急促，导致痉挛蔓延到肘部以上，就像严重手足搐搦症时那样。

<a id="S015"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** The chief trouble in a long experiment is that one tends to drop asleep and stop breathing, so a ruthless colleague is needed to prod one. Perhaps the oddest thing about such spasms is that they leave no bad after-effects, though it is true that certain signs of increased irritability of the nerves may persist for a fortnight.

**中文:** 长时间实验中的主要问题是，人容易入睡并停止呼吸，因此需要一位无情的同事来刺激实验。也许这种痉挛最奇怪的一点是，它们不会留下不良的后遗症，尽管神经过敏的某些迹象确实可能会持续两周。

<a id="S016"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** In our experiments on the effects of acids and alkalis on the human body, my colleague Dr. H. W. Davies and I made ourselves alkaline by over-breathing and by eating anything up to three ounces of bicarbonate of soda. We made ourselves acid by sitting in an airtight room with between six and seven per cent. of carbon dioxide in the air. This makes one breathe as if one had just completed a boat-race, and also gives one a rather violent headache. We analysed large amounts of blood and urine, and found out roughly what changes were occurring in them.

**中文:** 在我们关于酸和碱对人体影响的实验中，我和我的同事 H. W. Davies 博士通过过度呼吸和吃任何最多三盎司碳酸氢钠的东西使自己呈碱性。我们坐在一间密闭的房间里，浓度在百分之六到百分之七之间，让自己变得酸酸的。空气中的二氧化碳。这让人喘不过气来，就像刚刚完成一场划船比赛一样，而且还让人感到相当剧烈的头痛。我们分析了大量的血液和尿液，大致了解其中发生了哪些变化。

<a id="S017"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** But we still wanted something which would keep one acid for days at a time. Two hours was as long as any one wanted to stay in the carbon dioxide, even if the gas chamber at our disposal had not retained an ineradicable odour of ‘yellow cross gas’ from some wartime experiments, which made one weep gently every time one entered it. The most obvious thing to try was drinking hydrochloric acid. If one takes it strong it dissolves one’s teeth and burns one’s throat, whereas I wanted to let it diffuse gently all through my body. The strongest I ever cared to drink it was about one part of the commercial strong acid in a hundred of water, but a pint of that was enough for me, as it irritated my throat and stomach, while my calculations showed that I needed a gallon and a half to get the effect I wanted.

**中文:** 但我们仍然想要一种能够一次保留一种酸数天的东西。对于任何人来说，在二氧化碳里待上两个小时就足够了，即使我们手上的毒气室没有保留战时实验中那种让人每次一进去都会轻轻哭泣的“黄十字毒气”的难以消除的气味。最明显的尝试就是喝盐酸。如果一个人接受它很强烈，它会溶解一个人的牙齿并灼伤一个人的喉咙，而我想让它轻轻地扩散到我的全身。我喝过的最烈的酸大约是商业强酸的一部分溶于一百水，但一品脱对我来说就足够了，因为它会刺激我的喉咙和胃，而我的计算表明我需要一加仑半才能达到我想要的效果。

<a id="S018"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** I therefore had to think of a dodge for getting the hydrochloric acid in under false pretences. If one gives this acid to an animal it is not got rid of as such by the kidneys, as it would corrode the urinary passages; but about two-thirds (though not all) is neutralized by ammonia made in the body, and excreted as ammonium chloride. The same thing occurred in my own case. Now, in the chemical laboratory, when a reaction does not go all the way, it generally means that it can be reversed. For example, lime and chlorine dissolved in water combine to make chloride of lime, but there is always a little chlorine left over, which one can smell, and conversely one has only to dissolve chloride of lime in water for chlorine to be given off. So here I argued that if one ate ammonium chloride, it would partly break up in the body, liberating hydrochloric acid.

**中文:** 因此，我不得不想出一个办法，以虚假的借口将盐酸带入。如果将这种酸给予动物，肾脏不会将其排出，因为它会腐蚀尿道；但大约三分之二（尽管不是全部）被体内产生的氨中和，并以氯化铵的形式排出体外。同样的事情也发生在我自己的案例中。现在，在化学实验室中，当反应没有完全进行时，通常意味着它可以逆转。例如，石灰和氯溶解在水中生成石灰氯化物，但总有一点氯残留下来，人们可以闻到氯的气味，反之，只需将石灰氯化物溶解在水中即可释放出氯。所以我在这里提出，如果一个人吃了氯化铵，它会在体内部分分解，释放出盐酸。

<a id="S019"></a>
**Source:** https://jbshaldane.org/books/1927-Possible-Worlds/haldane-1927-possible-worlds.html

**Original:** This proved to be correct. As a matter of fact, the ammonium salts are poisonous when injected into the blood-stream, and the liver turns ammonia into a harmless substance called urea before it reaches the heart and brain on absorption from the gut. The hydrochloric acid is left behind and combines with sodium bicarbonate, which exists in all the tissues, producing sodium chloride and carbon dioxide. I have had this gas produced in me in this way at the rate of six quarts an hour (though not for an hour on end at that rate). Possibly my liver, had I been able to see it, would have resembled a Seidlitz powder, but even had I had a window through which to watch the process I should have been too busy breathing to pay much attention.
