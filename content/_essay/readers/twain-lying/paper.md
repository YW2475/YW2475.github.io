**Original:** Observe, I do not mean to suggest that the custom of lying has suffered any decay or interruption—no, for the Lie, as a Virtue, A Principle, is eternal; the Lie, as a recreation, a solace, a refuge in time of need, the fourth Grace, the tenth Muse, man's best and surest friend, is immortal, and cannot perish from the earth while this club remains. My complaint simply concerns the decay of the art of lying. No high-minded man, no man of right feeling, can contemplate the lumbering and slovenly lying of the present day without grieving to see a noble art so prostituted. In this veteran presence I naturally enter upon this theme with diffidence; it is like an old maid trying to teach nursery matters to the mothers in Israel. It would not become to me to criticise you, gentlemen—who are nearly all my elders—and my superiors, in this thing—if I should here and there seem to do it, I trust it will in most cases be more in a spirit of admiration than fault-finding; indeed if this finest of the fine arts had everywhere received the attention, the encouragement, and conscientious practice and development which this club has devoted to it, I should not need to utter this lament, or shed a single tear. I do not say this to flatter: I say it in a spirit of just and appreciative recognition. [It had been my intention, at this point, to mention names and to give illustrative specimens, but indications observable about me admonished me to beware of the particulars and confine myself to generalities.]

**中文:** 请注意，我并不是说，说谎的习惯有所衰退或中断——绝非如此。谎言作为一种美德、一项原则，是永恒的；作为一种消遣、一种慰藉、危难时的避难所，作为第四位美惠女神、第十位缪斯，以及人类最忠实可靠的朋友，它是不朽的。只要本俱乐部还在，它就绝不会从世上消失。我所抱怨的，只是说谎这门艺术的衰落。任何志趣高尚、感情健全的人，目睹当今那些笨拙草率的谎言，都会痛惜一门高贵的艺术竟遭到如此糟蹋。在诸位老手面前，我谈起这个题目，自然有些胆怯；这就像一位老姑娘试图向以色列的母亲们传授育儿之道。先生们，我不该批评诸位——你们几乎都比我年长，而且在这一道上比我高明；如果我偶尔显得像在批评，我想，那大多出于钦佩，而非挑剔。事实上，倘若这门最精妙的艺术，在各处都能得到本俱乐部给予它的那种重视、鼓励，以及认真的实践和发展，我就无须发出这番哀叹，也不必流下一滴眼泪。我这样说并非奉承，而是出于公正而由衷的赞赏。［我原打算在这里点出姓名，列举实例，但周围可见的种种迹象提醒我，最好避开细节，只谈一般情形。］

**Original:** No fact is more firmly established than that lying is a necessity of our circumstances—the deduction that it is then a Virtue goes without saying. No virtue can reach its highest usefulness without careful and diligent cultivation—therefore, it goes without saying that this one ought to be taught in the public schools—even in the newspapers. What chance has the ignorant uncultivated liar against the educated expert? What chance have I against Mr. Per—against a lawyer? Judicious lying is what the world needs. I sometimes think it were even better and safer not to lie at all than to lie injudiciously. An awkward, unscientific lie is often as ineffectual as the truth.

**中文:** 没有什么事实比说谎是我们所处环境的必然性更确定的了——由此推论说谎是一种美德，这是不言而喻的。任何美德如果不经过精心和勤奋的培养，就无法发挥最大作用——因此，不言而喻，这种美德应该在公立学校——甚至在报纸上——教授。无知的未受过教育的骗子有什么机会对抗受过教育的专家呢？我有什么机会对抗佩尔先生——对抗律师？世界需要明智的谎言。有时我认为根本不说谎比不明智地撒谎更好、更安全。尴尬的、不科学的谎言往往和真相一样无效。

**Source:** id00006

**Original:** Now let us see what the philosophers say. Note that venerable proverb: Children and fools always speak the truth. The deduction is plain —adults and wise persons never speak it. Parkman, the historian, says, "The principle of truth may itself be carried into an absurdity." In another place in the same chapters he says, "The saying is old that truth should not be spoken at all times; and those whom a sick conscience worries into habitual violation of the maxim are imbeciles and nuisances." It is strong language, but true. None of us could live with an habitual truth-teller; but thank goodness none of us has to. An habitual truth-teller is simply an impossible creature; he does not exist; he never has existed. Of course there are people who think they never lie, but it is not so—and this ignorance is one of the very things that shame our so-called civilization. Everybody lies—every day; every hour; awake; asleep; in his dreams; in his joy; in his mourning; if he keeps his tongue still, his hands, his feet, his eyes, his attitude, will convey deception—and purposely. Even in sermons—but that is a platitude.

**中文:** 现在让我们看看哲学家们怎么说。请注意那句古老的谚语：孩子和傻瓜总是说真话。推论很简单——成年人和智者从不说这句话。历史学家帕克曼说：“真理原则本身可能会变得荒谬。”在同一章节的另一处，他说：“俗话说，真理不应该在任何时候都说出来；那些因良心不安而担心习惯性违反这句格言的人是低能者和讨厌鬼。”这是很强烈的语言，但却是事实。我们谁都不能和一个习惯说真话的人一起生活。但谢天谢地，我们谁都不必这样做。习惯说真话的人简直是不可能的生物。他不存在；他从未存在过。当然，有些人认为自己从不撒谎，但事实并非如此——这种无知正是让我们所谓的文明蒙羞的事情之一。每个人都在说谎——每一天；每小时；醒;睡着了；在他的梦中；在他的喜乐中；在他的哀悼中；如果他保持舌头不动，他的手、他的脚、他的眼睛、他的态度就会传达欺骗——而且是故意的。即使是在布道中——但那是陈词滥调。

**Original:** In a far country where I once lived the ladies used to go around paying calls, under the humane and kindly pretence of wanting to see each other; and when they returned home, they would cry out with a glad voice, saying, "We made sixteen calls and found fourteen of them out" —not meaning that they found out anything important against the fourteen—no, that was only a colloquial phrase to signify that they were not at home—and their manner of saying it expressed their lively satisfaction in that fact. Now their pretence of wanting to see the fourteen—and the other two whom they had been less lucky with—was that commonest and mildest form of lying which is sufficiently described as a deflection from the truth. Is it justifiable? Most certainly. It is beautiful, it is noble; for its object is, not to reap profit, but to convey a pleasure to the sixteen. The iron-souled truth-monger would plainly manifest, or even utter the fact that he didn't want to see those people—and he would be an ass, and inflict totally unnecessary pain. And next, those ladies in that far country—but never mind, they had a thousand pleasant ways of lying, that grew out of gentle impulses, and were a credit to their intelligence and an honor to their hearts. Let the particulars go.

**中文:** 在我曾经生活过的一个遥远的国家，女士们常常打着想要见对方的人道和善意的幌子，四处拜访；当他们回到家时，他们会用高兴的声音喊道：“我们打了十六通电话，发现其中十四人出去了”——这并不是说他们发现了对这十四人有什么重要的事情——不，那只是一个口语短语，表示他们不在家——而他们说这句话的方式表达了他们对此事实的热烈满足。现在，他们假装想要见那十四个人——以及另外两个他们不太幸运的人——是最常见、最温和的谎言，这足以被描述为偏离事实。有道理吗？最肯定的是。它美丽，它高贵；因为它的目的不是为了获取利益，而是为了给十六人带来快乐。钢铁般的真相贩子会明目张胆地表现出来，甚至说出他不想见那些人的事实——他就会成为一个混蛋，并造成完全不必要的痛苦。接下来是那个遥远国家的那些女士——但没关系，她们有一千种令人愉悦的说谎方式，这些方式源于温柔的冲动，是她们智慧的荣耀，也是她们内心的荣耀。让细节过去吧。

**Original:** The men in that far country were liars, every one. Their mere howdy-do was a lie, because they didn't care how you did, except they were undertakers. To the ordinary inquirer you lied in return; for you made no conscientious diagnostic of your case, but answered at random, and usually missed it considerably. You lied to the undertaker, and said your health was failing—a wholly commendable lie, since it cost you nothing and pleased the other man. If a stranger called and interrupted you, you said with your hearty tongue, "I'm glad to see you," and said with your heartier soul, "I wish you were with the cannibals and it was dinner-time." When he went, you said regretfully, "Must you go?" and followed it with a "Call again;" but you did no harm, for you did not deceive anybody nor inflict any hurt, whereas the truth would have made you both unhappy.

**中文:** 那个遥远国家的人全都是骗子。他们仅仅打招呼就是一个谎言，因为他们不在乎你表现如何，除非他们是殡仪馆的承办人。对于普通询问者，你也撒谎；因为你没有认真地诊断你的病例，而是随意回答，而且通常会漏掉很多问题。你向殡仪馆的承办人撒谎，说你的健康状况每况愈下——这是一个完全值得称赞的谎言，因为它没有花费你任何钱，而且让对方很高兴。如果有陌生人打电话打断你，你会用热情的舌头说：“我很高兴见到你”，并用更热情的灵魂说：“我希望你和食人者在一起，而且现在是晚餐时间。”当他走的时候，你遗憾地说：“你一定要走吗？”然后接着说“再打一次电话”；但你并没有造成伤害，因为你没有欺骗任何人，也没有造成任何伤害，而真相会让你们俩都不高兴。

**Source:** id00010

**Original:** What I bemoan is the growing prevalence of the brutal truth. Let us do what we can to eradicate it. An injurious truth has no merit over an injurious lie. Neither should ever be uttered. The man who speaks an injurious truth lest his soul be not saved if he do otherwise, should reflect that that sort of a soul is not strictly worth saving. The man who tells a lie to help a poor devil out of trouble, is one of whom the angels doubtless say, "Lo, here is an heroic soul who casts his own welfare in jeopardy to succor his neighbor's; let us exalt this magnanimous liar."

**中文:** 我哀叹的是残酷的事实越来越普遍。让我们尽我们所能来根除它。有害的真相比有害的谎言没有任何价值。两者都不应该被说出。那些说出伤害性真理的人，以免他的灵魂如果不这样做就无法得救，他应该反思，这种灵魂严格来说不值得拯救。那个为了帮助可怜的魔鬼摆脱困境而说谎的人，天使无疑会说：“瞧，这是一个英雄的灵魂，他舍己救人，去救助邻居的福祉；让我们赞扬这个宽宏大量的骗子。”

**Original:** Among other common lies, we have the silent lie—the deception which one conveys by simply keeping still and concealing the truth. Many obstinate truth-mongers indulge in this dissipation, imagining that if they speak no lie, they lie not at all. In that far country where I once lived, there was a lovely spirit, a lady whose impulses were always high and pure, and whose character answered to them. One day I was there at dinner, and remarked, in a general way, that we are all liars. She was amazed, and said, "Not all?" It was before "Pinafore's" time so I did not make the response which would naturally follow in our day, but frankly said, "Yes, all—we are all liars. There are no exceptions." She looked almost offended, "Why, do you include me?" "Certainly," I said. "I think you even rank as an expert." She said "Sh-'sh! the children!" So the subject was changed in deference to the children's presence, and we went on talking about other things. But as soon as the young people were out of the way, the lady came warmly back to the matter and said, "I have made a rule of my life to never tell a lie; and I have never departed from it in a single instance." I said, "I don't mean the least harm or disrespect, but really you have been lying like smoke ever since I've been sitting here. It has caused me a good deal of pain, because I'm not used to it." She required of me an instance—just a single instance. So I said—

**中文:** 在其他常见的谎言中，我们有无声的谎言——一种通过简单地保持沉默和隐瞒真相来传达的欺骗。许多顽固的真理传播者沉迷于这种放纵，认为如果他们不说谎，他们就根本没有说谎。在我曾经生活过的那个遥远的国家里，有一位可爱的精灵，一位女士，她的冲动总是高尚而纯洁，她的性格也回应了这种冲动。有一天，我在那里吃晚饭，笼统地说，我们都是骗子。她惊讶道：“不是全部？”当时是在“Pinafore”时代之前，所以我没有做出我们这个时代自然会出现的反应，而是坦白地说：“是的，所有人——我们都是骗子，没有例外。”她看起来几乎被冒犯了，“为什么，你也包括我在内？” “当然，”我说。 “我觉得你甚至可以算是专家了。”她说：“嘘——嘘！孩子们！”所以考虑到孩子们的存在，我们改变了话题，然后继续谈论其他事情。但等年轻人一走开，这位女士就热情地回过头来，说道：“我给自己定下了一生不说谎的规矩，而且我从来没有背弃过这一点。”我说：“我没有丝毫的伤害或不尊重的意思，但实际上，自从我坐在这里以来，你一直在说谎，这让我很痛苦，因为我不习惯。”她需要我一个实例——只是一个实例。所以我说——

**Original:** "Well, here is the unfilled duplicate of the blank, which the Oakland hospital people sent to you by the hand of the sick-nurse when she came here to nurse your little nephew through his dangerous illness. This blank asks all manners of questions as to the conduct of that sick-nurse: 'Did she ever sleep on her watch? Did she ever forget to give the medicine?' and so forth and so on. You are warned to be very careful and explicit in your answers, for the welfare of the service requires that the nurses be promptly fined or otherwise punished for derelictions. You told me you were perfectly delighted with this nurse —that she had a thousand perfections and only one fault: you found you never could depend on her wrapping Johnny up half sufficiently while he waited in a chilly chair for her to rearrange the warm bed. You filled up the duplicate of this paper, and sent it back to the hospital by the hand of the nurse. How did you answer this question—'Was the nurse at any time guilty of a negligence which was likely to result in the patient's taking cold?' Come—everything is decided by a bet here in California: ten dollars to ten cents you lied when you answered that question." She said, "I didn't; I left it blank!" "Just so—you have told a silent lie; you have left it to be inferred that you had no fault to find in that matter." She said, "Oh, was that a lie? And how could I mention her one single fault, and she is so good?—It would have been cruel." I said, "One ought always to lie, when one can do good by it; your impulse was right, but your judgment was crude; this comes of unintelligent practice. Now observe the results of this inexpert deflection of yours. You know Mr. Jones's Willie is lying very low with scarlet-fever; well, your recommendation was so enthusiastic that that girl is there nursing him, and the worn-out family have all been trustingly sound asleep for the last fourteen hours, leaving their darling with full confidence in those fatal hands, because you, like young George Washington, have a reputa—However, if you are not going to have anything to do, I will come around to-morrow and we'll attend the funeral together, for, of course, you'll naturally feel a peculiar interest in Willie's case—as personal a one, in fact, as the undertaker."

**中文:** “嗯，这是空白的未填写的副本，是奥克兰医院的工作人员在生病的护士来到这里照顾你的小侄子度过他的危险疾病时，由她的手寄给你的。这个空白询问了有关那位生病的护士的行为的各种问题：“她是否曾经在值班时睡觉？她是否曾经忘记给过药？等等等等。警告您在回答时要非常小心和明确，因为服务的福利要求护士因失职而立即被罚款或以其他方式受到惩罚。你告诉我你对这位护士非常满意——她有一千个完美之处，只有一个缺点：你发现你永远不能指望她把约翰尼裹得严严实实，而他则坐在冰冷的椅子上等待她重新安排温暖的床。你填写了这张纸的副本，并由护士亲手将其寄回医院。你如何回答这个问题——“护士是否曾犯过可能导致病人感冒的疏忽？”来吧——一切都由加州的一个赌注决定：当你回答这个问题时，你撒了十美元到十美分。”她说：“我没有；我把它留空了！” “正是如此——你说了一个无声的谎言；你让人们推断你在这件事上没有发现任何过错。”她说：“哦，那是谎言吗？而且她这么好，我怎么能提到她的一个缺点呢？——那就太残酷了。”我说：“一个人应该总是撒谎，当一个人可以通过撒谎来做好事时；你的冲动是正确的，但你的判断是粗鲁的；这是不明智的做法造成的。现在观察一下你这种不熟练的偏转的结果。你知道琼斯先生的威利因猩红热而躺得很低；好吧，你的推荐是如此热情那个女孩正在那里护理他，疲惫不堪的一家人在过去的十四个小时里都在熟睡，让他们的宝贝充满信心地落在那双致命的手中，因为你，就像年轻的乔治·华盛顿一样，享有盛誉——但是，如果你不打算有任何事情可做，我明天会来，我们将一起参加葬礼，因为，当然，你自然会对威利的案子产生一种特殊的兴趣——事实上，作为私人案件，就像殡葬承办人。”

**Original:** Now, you see, this lady's fault was not in lying, but in lying injudiciously. She should have told the truth, there, and made it up to the nurse with a fraudulent compliment further along in the paper. **中文:** 现在，你看，这位女士的错不是撒谎，而是撒谎不明智。她应该在那里说实话，并在报纸后面用欺骗性的赞美来补偿护士。她本可以说：“从某一方面来说，这位生病的护士是完美的——当她值班时，她从不打鼾。”几乎任何令人愉快的小谎言都会消除这种麻烦但必要的真相表达的刺痛。

<a id="S015"></a>
**Source:** id00016

**Original:** Lying is universal—we all do it. Therefore, the wise thing is for us diligently to train ourselves to lie thoughtfully, judiciously; to lie with a good object, and not an evil one; to lie for others' advantage, and not our own; to lie healingly, charitably, humanely, not cruelly, hurtfully, maliciously; to lie gracefully and graciously, not awkwardly and clumsily; to lie firmly, frankly, squarely, with head erect, not haltingly, tortuously, with pusillanimous mien, as being ashamed of our high calling. Then shall we be rid of the rank and pestilent truth that is rotting the land; then shall we be great and good and beautiful, and worthy dwellers in a world where even benign Nature habitually lies, except when she promises execrable weather. Then—But I am but a new and feeble student in this gracious art; I cannot instruct this club.
